TL;DR

The rubric's class definitions were never the paper's. We transcribed Sarol Table 1 and added three clauses of our own — and two of them are inverted relative to how gold actually uses those labels. Those clauses were already in program-v0, so all five iterations hill-climbed on a wrong premise, and the two classes we mis-transcribed are exactly the two with the worst precision (NS 0.17, OVERSIMPLIFY 0.04).

So Plan A resets the definitions to the paper's verbatim wording, re-cuts the canonical baseline, and makes six prose changes that let the optimizer investigate rather than only rewrite — read the judge's traces, distrust its own inherited facts, report a defect instead of forcing an edit. Gold is the absolute target throughout. Evidence-acquisition programmability moved to Plan B; it needs driver stage coverage, not prose.

Contracts and invariants

The reset algorithm — exact, not "drop the accretion"

  1. Start from the program-v0 blobs of both operative files.
  2. Replace only the eight class-definition bullets with the paper's verbatim text.
  3. INDIRECT_NOT_REVIEW keeps a house definition, marked (house definition — not in Table 1). It is real in the gold data: 25 occurrences in claims-train.jsonl, 9 in claims-test.jsonl.
  4. Re-apply exactly one post-v0 clause: the ### Output contract evidence-array rule (adjudicator-dispatch-sarol.md:99, :107), independently required by validate_sarol.py:127. It recovered 5 MISSING_FIELD failures and is the only statistically real gain of the whole run.
  5. No other post-v0 clause is retained. Anything else worth keeping gets re-derived under measurement, not carried over on intuition.

Sarol MJ, Schneider J, Kilicoglu H. "Assessing citation integrity in biomedical publications: corpus annotation and NLP models." Bioinformatics 40(7):btae420, 2024. Definitions in §2.2 / Table 1. Open access: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231046/. Accessed 2026-09-09.

Step P0 — Paper-verbatim reset and canonical baseline recut

The three divergences, all our additions, all in the judge's read path:

paper sayswe saidcost
NOT_SUBSTANTIATE"the citation is relevant to the content … but fails to substantiate all statements""partial support but key element missing" we deleted relevance — the paper's actual NS-vs-IRRELEVANT boundary. 115 predictions, precision 0.17.

Step 0 — Gold is the absolute target

Your ruling, made the plan's opening instruction: gold is the objective, always. The nine label names and the 3-way collapse come from the benchmark and are frozen; every class definition is ours — a hypothesis about what gold means, not an authority. Where a definition and gold disagree, the definition is wrong.

The optimizer has full per-claim gold for the TRAIN batch and has never once derived a boundary empirically — it reasoned from inherited words. If it believes a specific gold label is indefensible it logs that one label with its evidence and moves on; it does not build rules around the belief that gold is wrong.

Step 1 — Ground blame in traces

The optimizer opened zero traces in five iterations, so it inferred "the judge skipped my rule" from wrong labels. The traces show 92% ordered-gate compliance and the forced-nuance artifact at 31/31. Four iterations went to hardening rules already being obeyed — 1,716 added words for a move inside the scatter.

New duty: open the trace before blaming non-compliance; if you cannot point to where the procedure was abandoned, it was not skipped. Sample correct-answer traces too. Fourth remedy added alongside the three additive ones: delete the competing guidance.

⚠ Codex caveat now in the plan: trace_ref may legitimately be null, and the mistake corpus lists only errors — correct-answer traces come from the per-iteration run_manifest.json. The prose names that artifact and a fallback.

Files: optimizer-instructions.md, failure-mode-discovery.md, subagent-blame-brief.md — the last two because the blame subagents are what read individual failures, which was your point.

Step 2 — A no-edit iteration is a terminal stop
Codex caught that my earlier version was impossible. commit_new_version stages only manifest entries and raises EmptyCommitError when none changed; loop.py:411 converts that to a terminal LoopStop. docs/journal/ is outside the manifest and the optimizer cannot commit. So "file a report and make no edit, that's a complete iteration" would have halted the run while producing no committed record.

Your ruling: terminal stop for human triage, no engine change. The optimizer writes its findings entry, states plainly that no program edit is warranted and why, and stops. The run halts for triage — a legitimate reportable outcome, not a failure. It does not invent a cosmetic edit to keep the loop alive.

The three reports/ files are gone; see the record-surface table above.

Step 3 — The evidence surface, named honestly

Your ruling stands — acquisition is the optimizer's problem, and the original paper-trail had an agent read the whole paper. The architecture already reflects that, and my earlier draft was false about where the lever is:

ACQUISITION_SCOPE is three prompts that are already manifest-owned and already optimizer-editable. The blocker is profiles.py:59-61 — the driver aborts extractor/verifier with STAGE_NOT_IMPLEMENTED. And BM25's fixity under retrieval is deliberate: it isolates judgment from acquisition for Phase 1.

So Plan A declines Codex's proposed fix (a versioned retrieval-config schema with query_strategy/k/ranker/bm25_k1/bm25_b) — it would engineer around a deliberate constraint and duplicate a Phase-2 capability. Retrieval latitude moves to Plan B, whose real content is driver stage coverage.

Step 4 — Trends, not single steps; and record the prediction

Your position, and the data agrees: v0→v5 is significant at p=0.021 while every individual step sits at p ≥ 0.69. So the guidance is trend framing, not a threshold that discards information. The scatter is stated as measured — a byte-identical program scored 0.48 and 0.42 on the same 50 claims, changing 16 of 50 labels.

The one thing a single sub-band step does not license is a reversal of direction — iteration 4 reversed the program's whole direction on a −0.02 move it had itself labelled inside the band. And the constraint loosens as TRAIN and VAL grow, so check the current n_total.

Step 5 — Bundle freely, but separably

Your economics: the sweep is ~$20 and the optimizer session ~$5, so forcing one edit per sweep multiplies the expensive thing to buy attribution of the cheap thing. The one-edit-per-iteration recommendation is dropped. What replaces it is a habit — target different label boundaries so per-class movement attributes them for free; isolate only after a bundle regresses; a repeat measurement of an unchanged program is a legitimate iteration.

Step 7 — Widen the surface SAFELY: split the driver

You said make the driver editable. Codex flagged that making the whole command editable also makes measurement and isolation contracts editable — argument parsing, the gold and source-paper prohibitions, one-dispatch/no-retry, stage topology, output ownership, abort semantics. An optimizer could raise its score or cut cost by weakening those while staying inside its declared scope. That is a reward-hacking surface, so the directive ships in its safe form:

  • Frozen shell — parsing/validation, the hard prohibitions, one dispatch no retry, output ownership, abort codes.
  • Narrowly editable component — which frozen prompt is dispatched and how slots are filled.
  • Invariant tests after every optimizer edit — a profile's stage count and the budget/release claims are lies if these can drift.

The judge reads only the enum and the rubric, so an editable definitions file that also duplicates the eight paper definitions becomes either inert or a silently drifting second authority.
